        The **Human Network Lab** studies the human brain and body as an interacting network — combining network neuroscience with research on brain-body interaction.

        <div style="max-width: 760px; margin: 2.5rem auto 0;">

        <div style="margin-bottom: 2.75rem;">
        <h2 style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem;">Brain network reorganization</h2>
        <p style="line-height: 1.65; margin-bottom: 0.7rem;">The brain is not a fixed wiring diagram. As cognitive demands shift — during learning, training, or changing task load — functional networks reconfigure, rebalancing integration and segregation. We track how this architecture rearranges over time, and what its flexibility tells us about behaviour.</p>
        <p style="font-size: 0.9rem; opacity: 0.65; margin: 0;">network neuroscience · neuroplasticity · modularity · integration and segregation · learning and training · time-varying dynamics · network geometry</p>
        </div>

        <div style="margin-bottom: 2.75rem;">
        <h2 style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem;">Brain–body interaction</h2>
        <p style="line-height: 1.65; margin-bottom: 0.7rem;">Cognition does not stop at the skull. Heartbeat, breath, and movement continuously shape neural dynamics, and the brain in turn regulates the body's rhythms. We treat the two as one interacting network rather than two separate systems.</p>
        <p style="font-size: 0.9rem; opacity: 0.65; margin: 0;">interoception · cardiac and respiratory coupling · autonomic dynamics · embodied cognition</p>
        </div>

        <div style="margin-bottom: 2.75rem;">
        <h2 style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem;">Polyrhythms in the human and the world</h2>
        <p style="line-height: 1.65; margin-bottom: 0.7rem;">Rhythms rarely run alone — they layer, drift, and interlock, in music, in neural oscillations, and in the body. Through entrainment, internal rhythms lock to external ones, from cortical oscillations down to heartbeat and breath. We ask whether entraining to several at once can sharpen attention, support motor learning, and benefit wellbeing.</p>
        <p style="font-size: 0.9rem; opacity: 0.65; margin: 0;">entrainment · neural oscillations · nested timescales · musical training · sensorimotor coordination · wellbeing</p>
        </div>

        <div style="margin-bottom: 2.75rem;">
        <h2 style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem;">Flow state</h2>
        <p style="line-height: 1.65; margin-bottom: 0.7rem;">Flow is the state of absorbed, effortless engagement in which action and awareness merge. Familiar as the experience is, its network signature remains poorly characterised. We look for the brain–body configurations that accompany flow, and the conditions that make it more likely.</p>
        <p style="font-size: 0.9rem; opacity: 0.65; margin: 0;">absorbed attention · effortless control · arousal regulation · optimal experience</p>
        </div>

        <div style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
        <h2 style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem;">Stress and resilience</h2>
        <p style="line-height: 1.65; margin-bottom: 0.7rem;">The same stressor leaves one person depleted and another strengthened. We approach resilience as a property of the whole brain–body network: how flexibly it reconfigures under load, and how readily it returns. That capacity is not shaped in isolation — it is embedded in the environments people inhabit, including their contact with the natural world.</p>
        <p style="font-size: 0.9rem; opacity: 0.65; margin: 0;">allostatic regulation · recovery dynamics · network flexibility · human ecology · nature exposure · individual differences</p>
        </div>

        </div>
